Unit V – Publication Misconduct

Ethics, Integrity & Academic Responsibility

📌 Important Points to Remember

  • FFP (Fabrication, Falsification, Plagiarism) = serious misconduct
  • Even small plagiarism can destroy your PhD
  • Authorship must be fair and transparent
  • Always disclose conflicts of interest
  • Use plagiarism tools before submission
  • Maintain research integrity at all stages

🎓 As a PhD Scholar – How to Start?

Start by understanding research ethics deeply. Before writing any paper, ensure originality. Use plagiarism tools like Turnitin. Learn authorship rules and discuss contributions clearly with your guide.

To become a strong ethical researcher:

  • Never copy content — always paraphrase and cite
  • Maintain raw data for verification
  • Follow ethical guidelines strictly
  • Study real misconduct cases
  • Use plagiarism reports to improve writing

🎤 Speak Like a “Pro Doctoral Fellow”

“When I began my PhD journey, I thought research was only about innovation. But I soon realized that integrity is the backbone of research.

Understanding publication misconduct changed my perspective. I learned that even unintentional plagiarism can damage years of hard work. I started using tools like Turnitin and ensured proper citation in every paper.

Today, I not only focus on producing research but on producing ethical research that stands strong under scrutiny and contributes genuinely to knowledge.”

📝 2 Marks Questions

Q1: What is plagiarism?
Using someone else's work without proper credit.
Q2: What is fabrication?
Making up fake data or results.
Q3: What is falsification?
Manipulating research data dishonestly.
Q4: What is authorship?
Recognition given to contributors of research work.
Q5: What is conflict of interest?
When personal interest affects research objectivity.
Q6: What is Turnitin?
A plagiarism detection software.
Q7: What is Urkund?
A tool to check plagiarism in academic work.
Q8: What are ethical issues?
Problems related to honesty and integrity in research.
Q9: What is research fraud?
Deliberate deception in research.
Q10: What is peer review?
Evaluation of research by experts.

📝 5 Marks Questions

Q1: Explain FFP.
Fabrication, falsification, and plagiarism are major forms of research misconduct that can lead to serious consequences.
Q2: Explain authorship issues.
Includes ghost authorship, gift authorship, and disputes over contribution.
Q3: Explain conflict of interest.
Occurs when personal or financial interests influence research outcomes.
Q4: Explain plagiarism detection tools.
Tools like Turnitin and Urkund check similarity and ensure originality.
Q5: Explain complaints and appeals.
Processes to report and resolve misconduct cases.
Q6: Explain research ethics.
Guidelines to ensure honesty and integrity in research.
Q7: Explain predatory practices.
Unethical practices like fake journals and misleading publications.
Q8: Explain plagiarism types.
Includes direct, self-plagiarism, and mosaic plagiarism.
Q9: Explain role of peer review.
Ensures quality and validity of research work.
Q10: Explain ethical responsibility of researcher.
Maintain honesty, transparency, and accountability.
