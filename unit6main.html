RPE Unit 6 – Databases & Research Metrics

Strategic Tools for High-Impact Research

📘 What is this Unit?

This unit focuses on research databases and metrics used to evaluate journals, articles, and researchers. It helps scholars understand where to publish and how research impact is measured.

🎯 Why should you study this?

  • To identify high-quality journals
  • To understand research impact
  • To improve your academic profile
  • To select the right publication platform

🎓 Importance for Ph.D Completion

Publishing in indexed journals (Scopus/WoS) is often mandatory for Ph.D. completion. Metrics like impact factor and h-index directly influence your academic career.

🚀 How to Become Expert?

  • Explore Scopus and Web of Science regularly
  • Check journal metrics before submission
  • Track your citations and h-index
  • Understand different metrics deeply

📚 Indexing & Citation Databases

Basic:

Databases store research articles and journals.

Advanced:

Indexing databases ensure journal quality, while citation databases track references and citations.

Examples:
  • Web of Science
  • Scopus
Real-Time Example:

Before submitting your paper, you verify whether the journal is indexed in Scopus.

Focus Points:
  • Indexing vs Citation databases
  • Journal credibility
  • Global recognition

📊 Journal Metrics (Impact Factor, CiteScore, etc.)

Basic:

Metrics evaluate the importance of journals.

Advanced:
  • Impact Factor (JCR)
  • CiteScore (Scopus)
  • SNIP (Source Normalized Impact)
  • SJR (SCImago Rank)
  • IPP (Impact per Publication)
Real-Time Example:

A journal with Impact Factor 5 is generally considered more influential than one with 1.5.

Focus Points:
  • Never rely on fake impact factors
  • Use official sources only

📈 Author Metrics (h-index, g-index, i10-index)

Basic:

Measures researcher productivity and impact.

Advanced:
  • h-index: Number of papers with equal citations
  • g-index: Gives more weight to highly cited papers
  • i10-index: Number of papers with 10+ citations
Real-Time Example:

If you have 10 papers each cited at least 10 times → h-index = 10.

Focus Points:
  • Focus on quality, not quantity
  • Increase citations through visibility

🌐 Altmetrics

Basic:

Measures online attention (social media, news, etc.).

Advanced:

Tracks impact beyond citations including Twitter mentions, blogs, and media coverage.

Real-Time Example:

Your paper is shared widely on Twitter → high altmetric score.

Focus Points:
  • Promote your research online
  • Engage with academic communities
