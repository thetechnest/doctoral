Ethics in Research

Not just knowledge. Integrity. Responsibility. Impact.

🌟 Why This Matters

In the world of research, your integrity is your identity. Without ethics, research becomes manipulation. Ethical research builds trust, credibility, and long-term impact. Every great researcher is remembered not for results alone, but for honesty.

📘 Core Concepts

Ethics in Research
Ethics ensures that research is conducted with honesty, transparency, and responsibility. It protects participants, maintains data integrity, and builds trust in scientific findings.
Intellectual Honesty
It means presenting truth without manipulation. A researcher must report findings exactly as they are, not as they wish them to be.
FFP (Falsification, Fabrication, Plagiarism)
Falsification = altering data
Fabrication = creating fake data
Plagiarism = stealing ideas
These destroy scientific credibility.
Redundant Publications
Duplicate publishing or salami slicing inflates academic records artificially. It misleads the research community.
Selective Reporting
Showing only positive results creates bias. True research reports both success and failure.

🧠 Summary

Ethics is not optional in research — it is the foundation. A researcher without integrity may succeed temporarily, but will fail permanently.

🎯 5 Marks Questions

Explain ethics in research.
Ethics in research refers to moral principles guiding research practices. It ensures honesty, transparency, and responsibility. Ethical research avoids bias, protects participants, and produces reliable knowledge.
Explain intellectual honesty.
Intellectual honesty involves presenting ideas truthfully without distortion. It builds trust and credibility in research work.
Explain FFP.
FFP stands for falsification, fabrication, and plagiarism. These are major forms of scientific misconduct that damage research integrity.
Explain redundant publication.
Redundant publication involves publishing the same work multiple times. It misleads the academic community and inflates research output.
Explain selective reporting.
Selective reporting means presenting only favorable data. It leads to biased conclusions and reduces research reliability.

⚡ 2 Marks Questions

What is plagiarism?
Plagiarism is copying others' work without credit. It is unethical.
What is fabrication?
Fabrication is creating fake data. It is a serious violation.
What is falsification?
Falsification is altering data to mislead results. It damages research credibility.
What is research ethics?
It is the moral framework guiding research. It ensures honesty and integrity.
What is salami slicing?
It is dividing one study into multiple papers. It is unethical.
