🎯 5 Marks Questions (With Answers)

1. Explain research objectives, types of research and knowledge economy.
Research is a systematic process aimed at generating new knowledge or solving problems. The objectives of research include discovering new facts, verifying existing knowledge, and developing innovative solutions. Types of research include basic research (knowledge-oriented) and applied research (problem-solving). In a knowledge economy, research plays a vital role as innovation and information become key economic drivers, helping industries and societies grow through intellectual contributions.
2. Discuss stakeholders’ perspectives in research.
Stakeholders in research include researchers, industries, government, and society, each having different expectations. Researchers focus on knowledge creation, industries aim for practical applications, while governments look for policy development and societal benefits. Understanding stakeholder perspectives ensures that research is relevant, funded, and impactful, aligning outcomes with real-world needs.
3. Explain research design and characteristics of good research.
Research design is the blueprint or plan used to conduct a study, outlining methods, data collection, and analysis. A good research study is systematic, logical, objective, and reproducible. It ensures accuracy, reliability, and validity of results. Proper design helps in minimizing bias and achieving meaningful conclusions.
4. Explain hypothesis formulation and testing.
A hypothesis is a testable statement predicting a relationship between variables. It is formulated based on prior knowledge and research questions. Testing involves collecting data and using statistical methods to accept or reject the hypothesis. This process ensures scientific validation and supports evidence-based conclusions.
5. Describe literature search techniques.
Literature search involves identifying and analyzing existing research related to a topic. Techniques include keyword search, backward search (checking references), and forward search (finding citing papers). These methods help researchers understand previous work, avoid duplication, and identify research gaps.
6. Explain ICT tools and research databases.
ICT tools such as digital libraries, search engines, and academic databases like IEEE, Scopus, and Google Scholar enable easy access to global research. These tools help in data collection, analysis, and collaboration, improving research efficiency and quality.
7. Discuss meta-analysis and literature review.
Literature review involves summarizing and analyzing existing research studies, while meta-analysis statistically combines results from multiple studies to draw stronger conclusions. Both are essential for understanding research trends and identifying gaps for future studies.
8. Explain research methods: theoretical, experimental, empirical.
Theoretical research focuses on developing concepts and models, experimental research involves controlled testing in labs, and empirical research is based on real-world observations and data. Each method serves different research purposes depending on the problem and objectives.
9. Explain Intellectual Property Rights.
Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) protect creations of the human mind such as inventions, designs, and artistic works. It includes patents, copyrights, trademarks, and geographical indications. IPR encourages innovation by providing legal protection and recognition to creators.
10. Explain research topic selection and formulation of research statement.
Selecting a research topic involves identifying a relevant, feasible, and interesting problem area. A research statement clearly defines the objective and scope of the study. It should be precise, focused, and aligned with research goals to guide the entire research process.

⚡ 2 Marks Questions (With Answers)

1. Define research.
Research is a systematic investigation to discover new knowledge. It aims to solve problems and validate existing theories.
2. What is hypothesis?
A hypothesis is a testable statement about a relationship between variables. It guides the research process.
3. Define knowledge economy.
A knowledge economy is driven by information and innovation. It relies on intellectual capabilities rather than physical inputs.
4. What is literature review?
It is a study of existing research on a topic. It helps identify gaps and understand prior work.
5. What is plagiarism?
Plagiarism is copying others' work without credit. It is considered unethical in research.
6. What is patent?
A patent gives exclusive rights to an invention. It prevents others from using it without permission.
7. Define research design.
Research design is the plan for conducting a study. It outlines methods and procedures.
8. What is meta-analysis?
Meta-analysis combines results from multiple studies. It provides stronger conclusions.
9. What is keyword search?
Keyword search uses specific terms to find relevant information. It helps locate research articles quickly.
10. What is ICT tool in research?
ICT tools are digital technologies used in research. They help in data collection, analysis, and communication.
