Module 3 – Publication Ethics & Misconducts

PhD Survival Guide: Ethics First

📌 Important Points to Remember

  • FFP = Fabrication, Falsification, Plagiarism (VERY IMPORTANT)
  • Even small plagiarism can cancel your PhD
  • Always cite properly
  • Follow COPE & WAME guidelines
  • Never manipulate data
  • Avoid predatory journals

🎓 As a PhD Scholar – How to Start?

Start by understanding research integrity deeply. Before writing any paper, ensure originality. Use plagiarism tools like Turnitin. Learn ethical guidelines and always maintain transparency.

  • Write original content
  • Use reference managers
  • Check plagiarism before submission
  • Understand authorship rules
  • Verify journal authenticity

🚀 How to Become an Excellent Scholar?

  • Focus on ethical research practices
  • Maintain data transparency
  • Follow global standards (COPE)
  • Publish in genuine journals
  • Build trust in research community

🎤 Speak Like a Pro Doctoral Fellow

“When I started my PhD, I believed research was only about innovation. But I soon realized that integrity is more important than results.

I learned that even a small act like copying a paragraph without citation can destroy years of work. So I started using plagiarism tools and strictly followed ethical guidelines.

Today, I focus not only on producing research but on producing honest, credible, and impactful research.”

🌍 Real-Time Example

A famous researcher lost their reputation after falsifying data in published papers. Their work was retracted, and they were banned from publishing. This shows how serious misconduct is.

📊 Summary

Publication ethics ensures honesty and trust in research. Misconduct such as plagiarism, falsification, and predatory publishing can damage careers. Ethical research is essential for PhD success.

🤔 Points to Ponder

  • Is my work completely original?
  • Did I cite all sources properly?
  • Is my data genuine and unaltered?
  • Is the journal trustworthy?

📝 2 Marks Questions

Q1: What is research ethics?
Principles ensuring honesty and integrity in research work.
Q2: What is plagiarism?
Copying others’ work without proper credit.
Q3: What is fabrication?
Creating fake data.
Q4: What is falsification?
Altering data dishonestly.
Q5: What is COPE?
Committee on Publication Ethics.
Q6: What is WAME?
World Association of Medical Editors.
Q7: What is conflict of interest?
Bias due to personal interest.
Q8: What is duplicate publication?
Publishing same work multiple times.
Q9: What is predatory journal?
Fake journal without proper review.
Q10: What is research integrity?
Honesty in conducting research.

📝 5 Marks Questions

Q1: Explain research ethics.
Research ethics ensures honesty, transparency, and accountability in research work, preventing misconduct.
Q2: Explain FFP.
Fabrication, falsification, and plagiarism are serious misconduct forms that harm research credibility.
Q3: Explain publication ethics.
Guidelines ensuring ethical publishing practices and maintaining research integrity.
Q4: Explain predatory journals.
Journals that exploit authors without proper peer review or quality checks.
Q5: Explain authorship issues.
Problems like ghost authorship and unfair credit distribution.
Q6: Explain conflict of interest.
Bias due to personal or financial interests affecting research.
Q7: Explain redundant publication.
Publishing same data in multiple journals.
Q8: Explain selective reporting.
Reporting only favorable results.
Q9: Explain complaints and appeals.
Processes to report and resolve misconduct.
Q10: Explain plagiarism detection.
Using tools to identify copied content.
