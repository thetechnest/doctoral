Motivation
The rapid adoption of cloud-based AI has intensified concerns related to data privacy, regulatory compliance, and trust. Centralized learning models are increasingly incompatible with sensitive and distributed data ecosystems such as healthcare, finance, and government platforms.
Research Gap
  • Inadequate privacy guarantees in standard federated learning
  • High computational cost of cryptographic techniques
  • Limited adaptive mechanisms in multi-cloud environments
  • Lack of deployable, end-to-end secure FL frameworks
Core Research Contribution
A unified, adaptive federated cloud AI framework integrating Differential Privacy and Homomorphic Encryption to provide end-to-end privacy, secure aggregation, and scalable deployment.
Research GOAL
Organizations across healthcare, finance, and governance depend on AI but face data-sharing restrictions.
Current privacy-preserving methods either:
Reduce accuracy (Differential Privacy) or
Increase computation time (Homomorphic Encryption).
Need: A hybrid framework that ensures privacy, efficiency, and scalability.
My goal is to make federated learning practical for multi-cloud AI collaboration under strict data regulations.
Experimental Setup and Multi-Cloud Deployment
The proposed framework will be deployed and tested across real-world cloud platforms, including Amazon Web Services (AWS), Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform (GCP). This phase involves configuring compute resources, networking, and security services across clouds, and simulating realistic federated learning scenarios with distributed clients. Container orchestration and cloud-native monitoring tools will be used to manage experiments and collect performance data.
Evaluation and Performance Analysis
The final phase involves comprehensive evaluation of the proposed framework against existing federated learning baselines. The evaluation will consider multiple metrics, including model accuracy, convergence time, communication overhead, privacy guarantees, and system scalability. Experimental results will be analyzed to assess the effectiveness of adaptive mechanisms and the impact of privacy-preserving techniques under varying workloads and cloud configurations.
Scope and Applications
The proposed framework is applicable to privacy-sensitive domains such as healthcare analytics, financial fraud detection, smart cities, and cross-enterprise AI collaboration.

Problem Statement

How can federated cloud AI systems be designed to ensure strong, provable privacy guarantees while maintaining model accuracy, scalability, and practical deployability across heterogeneous multi-cloud infrastructures?

Research Objectives

  • Analyze privacy vulnerabilities in federated learning systems
  • Design adaptive privacy-budget mechanisms
  • Incorporate homomorphic encryption for secure aggregation
  • Develop a scalable multi-cloud federated architecture
  • Evaluate privacy–utility–performance trade-offs

Proposed Methodology

Proposed Work and Architecture

The proposed work focuses on designing an adaptive, privacy-preserving federated cloud AI architecture that enables secure learning across multiple cloud environments without exposing sensitive local data. The framework integrates Differential Privacy and Homomorphic Encryption to provide dual-layer protection during model training and aggregation.

The architecture consists of distributed client nodes deployed across heterogeneous cloud platforms, a secure aggregation layer, and an adaptive coordination module that manages privacy budgets, model updates, and communication efficiency.

Figure: Proposed Adaptive and Privacy-Preserving Federated Cloud AI Architecture
  • Local training is performed independently at each cloud node.
  • Differential Privacy ensures protection against inference attacks.
  • Homomorphic Encryption enables secure aggregation over encrypted updates.
  • The aggregator constructs a global model without accessing raw data.
  • The updated global model is redistributed for iterative learning.
